The Haunting of Hill House

★★★★☆ 8.6 / 10

The Haunting of Hill House is a masterpiece of modern horror television. Created by Mike Flanagan and based on Shirley Jackson's novel, the series tells the story of the Crain family, whose lives were shattered by their summer in Hill House. The series moves between two timelines — the terrifying events in the house and the damaged adults the children became.

What makes Hill House extraordinary is its focus on character and grief. The horror is real and effective — the Bent-Neck Lady is one of the most iconic horror images in recent memory, and the hidden ghosts in every frame reward repeated viewing. But the scares serve the story, not the other way around. The show is about trauma, addiction, mental illness, and the ways family bonds can both save and destroy us.

The cast is exceptional. The younger actors playing the children are as good as the adults, which is essential for a story that relies on both timelines. Victoria Pedretti, Oliver Jackson-Cohen, Kate Siegel, and Elizabeth Reaser each carry the weight of their characters' trauma with heartbreaking authenticity. The final episode, a eulogy that redefines the meaning of the story, is one of television's great achievements.

Flanagan's direction is masterful. The long takes, particularly the funeral home sequence, demonstrate technical brilliance while serving the emotional story. The series understands that true horror comes from loss, guilt, and the things we cannot say to the people we love.

The Haunting of Hill House is essential for anyone who believes horror can be art. It's terrifying, heartbreaking, and ultimately hopeful.
