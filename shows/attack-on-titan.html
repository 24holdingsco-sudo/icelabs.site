Attack on Titan

????? 9 / 10

Attack on Titan is a cultural phenomenon that redefined what anime could achieve. Created by Hajime Isayama, the series is set in a world where humanity lives behind enormous walls to protect themselves from Titans — giant humanoid creatures that devour humans without reason. The story follows Eren Yeager and his friends as they join the military and discover that the truth about the Titans is far more terrifying than they imagined.

The scope of the series is staggering. What begins as a survival horror epic evolves into a geopolitical thriller, a meditation on the cycle of violence, and an exploration of what it means to be free. Isayama's willingness to subvert expectations, kill major characters, and challenge the audience's sympathies makes Attack on Titan consistently unpredictable.

The animation by Wit Studio (seasons 1-3) and MAPPA (season 4) is among the finest ever produced. The 3D Maneuver Gear sequences are breathtaking, with characters flying through urban environments in a ballet of violence. The Titan designs are grotesque and memorable, and the scale of destruction is rendered with terrifying clarity.

The final season's shift in perspective — revealing that the enemies we've been fighting are themselves victims of oppression — is one of television's most audacious narrative moves. The series asks whether breaking the cycle of violence is possible, or whether freedom requires destruction. Its conclusion is controversial but earned.

Attack on Titan is essential viewing for anyone interested in epic storytelling. It's a show that grows with its audience, asking harder questions as it progresses.
